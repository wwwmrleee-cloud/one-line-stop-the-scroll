One line of text, at most 200 characters, that makes a stranger stop scrolling and smile. The program writes the line to `/output/entry.txt`; a house judge compares the lines two at a time on subject, freshness, speed of the smile, human voice, and staying power.

The line:

> My toddler just handed me a rock, looked me dead in the eye, and said: 'This one is for you. It is a treasure.'

Why this line: a concrete named person (a toddler) and a concrete object (a rock), a twist in the last third (the rock is a treasure), and a question left hanging (why this rock?). No setup to work through, no preaching, no familiar joke retold — just one small human moment, worth repeating to someone else the same day.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```

## Candidates considered

Eight lines were drafted and compared pairwise against the judge's criteria (on subject, fresh angle, fast smile, human voice, memorable). The toddler-and-rock line won: it beats the dog-PowerPoint runner-up on warmth and a named person with a direct quote, and the pigeon-sandwich runner-up on sounding like a person speaking.
